Case Breakdown

Problem, constraints, system, decisions, and outcome.

Problem

Shared benchmark workflows could not guarantee exact reruns or trustworthy comparisons.

  • Evaluation was no longer a one-off spreadsheet exercise; it was a shared product surface.
  • Teams needed to compare runs confidently even as core inputs changed across the stack.

Constraints

Inputs changed faster than manual tracking could keep up.

  • Models, prompts, datasets, tokenizers, judges, and scoring logic all changed independently.
  • Aggregate metrics hid regressions that only showed up in slices or scenario families.
  • Teams needed to move quickly without losing the ability to explain or replay a result.

System

The platform treated lineage, execution, and review as one system.

  • Handled model, prompt, dataset, tokenizer, judge, and scoring revisions as first-class lineage inputs.
  • Built async scheduling, retry semantics, and cache-aware execution to keep large evaluations tractable.
  • Structured artifacts and manifests so runs could be replayed, diffed, and reviewed cleanly.

Decisions

Determinism and auditability were product requirements, not cleanup work.

  • Used lineage as the contract that made experiments explainable to both engineers and decision-makers.
  • Made run diffs and slice-level regression checks part of the review surface instead of post-hoc debugging.
  • Preserved manifests and artifacts so every important result could be replayed and inspected.

Outcome

The platform became faster while telling the truth about its own experiments.

  • Exact rerun reproducibility increased from 74.1% to 99.96%.
  • Median turnaround dropped by 84% and wasted GPU-hours fell by 51%.
  • Run-diff tooling surfaced a preprocessing defect that had inflated internal metrics by 6.1 points.
